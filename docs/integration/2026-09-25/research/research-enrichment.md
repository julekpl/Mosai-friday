## D3 - which company-enrichment provider (if any)

**Option A - No provider (first-party scan + schema.org + Google Places), recommended for launch.**
- Pros: no per-signup vendor cost beyond Places calls; no new DPA/vendor risk; matches "truth" principle (only shows what MOSAI itself confirmed); works day one for NL/PL/DE/UK/US alike.
- Cons: weaker industry/description quality than a real enrichment API; Google Places pricing has gotten more expensive and contact-field caching terms need a legal read; needs the user's own site URL or domain to scan (no scan possible for a brand-new business with no site yet).
- Best paired with: national registry lookup (KVK/Companies House) for legal name/address ground truth where available, since those are free/near-free and authoritative, and Brandfetch's free logo tier for a logo with no incremental cost.

**Option B - Add a paid B2B enrichment API (Apollo, CompanyEnrich, or PDL) as a fallback when the scan+registry path returns too little.**
- CompanyEnrich is the cheapest per-call option and needs no upfront seat commitment ($0.001-0.01/call), best if D3 goes paid.
- PDL is a reasonable second choice with a genuine free tier (100/mo) and clear per-record pricing, but its enterprise jump ($5,000/mo) is steep if volume grows past the low tiers.
- Apollo and Clearbit/Breeze are the weakest fits: both require an ongoing seat/subscription unrelated to per-signup usage, which is a bad shape for a self-serve signup flow that must scale with free-tier and paid-tier signups independently of any human "seat."
- Crustdata is not viable for D3 given no public pricing and a sales-gated model.

**Recommendation for D3:** Start with Option A (no paid provider) for MVP, and register CompanyEnrich as the D3 fallback provider to add later behind a feature flag once volume/accuracy data shows the free path under-delivers. Do not adopt Apollo, Clearbit/Breeze or Crustdata - wrong pricing shape (seat-gated or sales-gated) for a per-signup unit-economics decision. Confirm CompanyEnrich's DPA and data-sourcing/GDPR posture directly with the vendor before any contract (currently unverified in this research pass).

## D11 - spending cap

Cost estimates below assume Option A (Places calls only, ~$0.02-0.025/call at the "contact data" tier when a phone/site lookup is needed) or the CompanyEnrich fallback for the minority of signups that need it. Assume ~20% of signups need the paid fallback (site had no schema.org data, no matching Places listing).

| Volume/mo | Places-only cost (Option A, 100% of signups) | + CompanyEnrich fallback (20% of signups, Starter tier $0.0098/call) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 signups | ~$2-2.50 | + ~$0.20 = ~$2.70 |
| 1,000 signups | ~$20-25 | + ~$2 = ~$27 |
| 10,000 signups | ~$200-250 | + ~$20 (at Scale tier pricing, cheaper) = ~$220-270 |

**Recommendation for D11:** Set a hard monthly cap at roughly 1.5x the 10,000-signup estimate above (~$400/mo) with a per-signup ceiling of a single Places call + a single fallback-provider call (no retries beyond one), auto-disable enrichment for the rest of the billing period if the cap is hit, and alert the owner rather than silently degrading to fake data. Revisit the cap number once real signup volume and hit-rate on the free path are known - these are estimates from public pricing pages, not measured MOSAI traffic.

## D12 - whether to look up a signup email's domain automatically

**Recommendation: yes, but narrowly scoped and disclosed.**
- Do it: strip the domain from the work email, run the free path (schema.org scan of `https://<domain>`, national registry lookup by name match, Google Places by domain/name) automatically at signup, since this directly serves the onboarding promise (prefilled business profile) and uses only data the business itself published.
- Do not: auto-run a paid third-party enrichment call (Apollo/CompanyEnrich/PDL) without it being covered by D11's cap and ideally gated to only fire when the free path returns nothing, to keep per-signup cost near zero for the common case.
- Skip entirely for free/personal email domains (gmail.com, outlook.com, etc.) - detect via a common free-mail-domain list and skip lookup, since there is no company to resolve and it would waste calls/budget.
- GDPR angle: this is business/company data, not personal data about the signup email's owner (no person enrichment per the brief), which keeps this closer to legitimate interest without needing separate consent, but confirm with the owner given "money, tax, legal wording" and GDPR judgment calls belong to them per AGENTS.md §7.
